# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Budget Buddy is for young students and other people managing tight monthly cash flow who want help checking impulse purchases before they commit. They may be tired, stressed, or unsure whether a purchase is reasonable, and they need a clear answer that respects their situation without making them feel judged.

## Product Purpose

Budget Buddy helps people pause before spending, understand how a purchase fits into their monthly obligations, and make a more confident decision. Success means the user feels oriented quickly: they can enter basic budget details, ask a plain-language spending question, and get practical guidance they can act on.

## Brand Personality

Welcoming, simple, personable.

The voice should feel like a thoughtful friend who is good with money: direct, calm, and kind. It should use simple language, avoid financial jargon, and make the user feel capable rather than scolded.

## Anti-references

Budget Buddy should not feel like a bank dashboard, a dense budgeting spreadsheet, a credit-card upsell, or a shame-based finance coach. Avoid intimidating fintech polish, generic navy-and-gold money branding, hustle-culture advice, and copy that makes users feel irresponsible for wanting things.

## Design Principles

1. Make the pause feel friendly: the interface should slow impulse decisions without making the user feel blocked or judged.
2. Speak plainly: every label, prompt, and recommendation should be understandable on the first read.
3. Show the tradeoff: connect spending advice to the user's actual monthly cash picture, not abstract financial rules.
4. Protect dignity: advice should be honest but never scolding, moralizing, or patronizing.
5. Keep the brand human: visual and interaction choices should feel warm, personal, and approachable rather than institutional.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for text and controls. Use reduced-motion-safe interaction patterns. Do not rely on green and red alone to communicate spending recommendations; pair color with text, icons, and structure. Keep language simple for users who may be stressed, distracted, or new to budgeting.
